= Auctions in euros with direct payment
:description: How a member lists an auction in euros, how the winner pays the seller directly, what the administrator switches on, and what Lapis Cloud does and does not do.
:toc:
:icons: font

NOTE: Wave V1.9.320. **Off by default and not legally reviewed.** An administrator must switch it on after reading a legal notice; recommended only for a small pilot. The technical background is in xref:../architecture/auction-fiat-and-federation.adoc[auction-fiat-and-federation.adoc].

== What it is

An auction can run in **euros** instead of LTR. Lapis Cloud then only establishes *who* won and *at which price*. **It does not receive, hold, forward or refund any money, and it never books LTR for such an auction.** The winner pays the seller directly (bank transfer or the seller's own payment link). Lapis Cloud offers no escrow, no buyer protection and no dispute handling.

== For members

*Listing.* In the form "Neues Angebot" a choice *Bezahlung* appears when the administrator has switched euro auctions on. Choose *Euro (Direktzahlung)*:

. Enter the starting price (1.00 to 100,000.00 EUR, at most two decimals) and optionally a buy-now price above it.
. Enter your **payment details**: account holder, a German IBAN and/or an `https` payment link (at least one of them). They are stored encrypted and shown only to the highest bidder after the award. The server can replace them before the award, but the app has no screen for it yet.
. The first time, read and acknowledge the *notice for sellers*. A new version of the notice has to be acknowledged again before the next listing.
. There is **no listing fee** in LTR. At most 20 euro auctions of one seller can be open at the same time.

*Bidding.* A bid is a maximum in euros. Nothing is reserved; the highest bidder wins at the second-highest maximum plus 1.00 EUR (or at the starting price without competition). *Buy now* works as for LTR.

*After the award* the auction shows a settlement panel to the seller and the winner only:

* The **winner** opens the payment details ("Zahlungsangaben anzeigen"), copies the IBAN and the payment reference or opens the payment link (the host of the link is shown next to the button) and, once the goods arrived, confirms "Wareneingang".
* The **seller** confirms "Zahlungseingang" when the money arrived.
* Each confirmation is final. After both, the auction is *Abgeschlossen (bezahlt und geliefert)*. If the winner never pays, the auction stays *Zuschlag erteilt, Zahlung offen*: there is no cancellation, no deadline and no reminder.

The server allows changing the currency and the prices of an own auction **before the first bid** (euro to LTR charges the usual LTR listing fee, once per auction). **Server API only: the app has no screen for it yet.**

*Privacy of the progress.* Everybody sees that an auction was awarded; whether it is already paid or completed is visible only to the seller and the winner (others see "awarded").

== Price offers

A seller of a euro auction **with a buy-now price** can allow *Preisvorschläge* when listing (a checkbox; it cannot be changed afterwards, and the terms of the auction are frozen once the first offer exists). A buyer proposes a price; it binds the buyer only once the seller accepts.

* The proposal must be at least the visible price plus 1.00 EUR (the starting price without a bid) and below the buy-now price. A proposal never extends the auction.
* It is valid for 48 hours, but never beyond the end of the auction. The screen shows the real deadline.
* The seller *accepts* (the auction ends at that price and goes on as after any award: payment directly, confirmations), *declines*, or makes **one** counter-offer, which the buyer accepts or declines. There is no free text.
* Limits: one open proposal per buyer and auction; at most **10 open proposals per auction** (counter-offers and closed proposals do not count, so the seller has to answer before an eleventh buyer can speak up; the seller sees "Offene Preisvorschläge: n von 10"); at most five proposals of one buyer per auction in total (withdrawn ones included); a hard cap of 200 rows per auction.
* **Block after three declines:** when the seller has declined three proposals of one buyer, that buyer cannot make a new proposal on **that auction** for **7 days**. The buyer sees a lock and the end of the block (no counter before it); the seller sees "Ablehnungen: n von 3" per buyer and is warned in the dialog of the third decline. After the end a new cycle starts from zero. A declined counter-offer, a withdrawal and a lapse do not count. The seller cannot lift a block early.
* **Withdrawal:** the author of an open proposal can withdraw it until the answer has arrived (the buyer is told that he can then make a new one, unless he is blocked). If the seller answered meanwhile, the buyer is told how (accepted, declined, countered, expired).
* **Notice with a version:** the notice above the "send" button ("A price suggestion only binds you once the seller accepts it ...") comes from the server, is shown unchanged and has a version. The version, the language and a SHA-256 of the exact wording are stored with each proposal and counter-offer, so it can be shown which wording the author saw. Only German and English exist; every other interface language shows the English text.
* When the auction ends (time, buy now or an accepted proposal) every other open proposal ends with it ("Auktion beendet").
* Price bounds: a proposal must exceed the visible price by the minimum increment when bids exist. The seller may still accept, or counter at, an amount equal to the visible price (the visible price may have risen to a proposal's amount after it was made); only an amount below it is refused.
* A buyer sees only his own proposals and the counter-offers addressed to him (*Meine Preisvorschläge*); the seller sees the list of his auction with the names of the proposers. Nobody else sees any figure about the proposals.
* Not legally reviewed: when an offer binds, and consumer rights against a commercial seller, are open legal questions.

== For administrators

*Administration > Auction > Auctions in euros (direct payment).* *Euro-Auktionen aktivieren* shows the operator notice (version and hash are echoed back unchanged) and needs the explicit confirmation; the acknowledgment (who, when, which version) is recorded. *Deaktivieren* blocks only new euro listings; running euro auctions can still be bid on, awarded and confirmed.

The feature needs `LAPIS_SECRET_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (the key that also protects the SEPA and travel-expense IBANs): without it no euro auction can be created. Backups taken before schema V166 (and, since price offers, before V167) cannot be restored.

== Auctions from partner servers

When the administrator switches the exchange on and the auction module is on, an active member sees below the own list a section "Auktionen von Partnerservern": short hints of auctions that partner servers offer (title, price with its age, Sofortkauf price, end, the badge "Preisvorschlag möglich"). A card has one link that opens the auction on the offering server; **bidding and buying are only possible there**, never here. The card carries the host of the offering server first; the price of a running auction is refreshed at most every 30 minutes and its age is shown. A hint disappears at the moment its auction ends. Guests, friends and donors do not see the section. An administrator can hide a hint; it then does not come back, also not if the partner server reports it again. The feature is off by default and has not been legally reviewed. Operators: see xref:auction-federation-runbook.adoc[the runbook].

== Data protection

The payment details of the seller are deleted at once when the auction closes without a sale, 30 days after the later confirmation, 180 days after the award if it never completes, and when the seller is erased. They are in no list, no audit entry and no log. A seller's consent to show an auction to partner servers is part of his data export; when he is erased, his hints are withdrawn from the partners first and the consent ends. See xref:../architecture/dsgvo.adoc[dsgvo.adoc].

== Limits

* EUR only; Germany first (a German IBAN is required).
* No appearance on partner servers (a member can offer an own euro auction to partners, see below), no outbid or ending-soon dashboard signals for euro auctions (the won and sold signals exist).
* The wording of both notices is the proposal of the authors and has **not** been checked by a lawyer.
