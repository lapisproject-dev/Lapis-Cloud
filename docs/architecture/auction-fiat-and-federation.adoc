= Auctions in fiat currency and in the federation (concept)
:description: Concept for auctions paid in euros or another currency without Lapis Cloud ever holding money, buy now and price offers, and showing auctions to partner servers.
:toc:
:toc-placement: preamble
:icons: font

WARNING: **Wave (a) "currency per auction and direct payment, including buy now in fiat" is implemented (V1.9.320, schema V166); everything else in this document is still concept.** The feature is **off by default** (instance switch) and **has not been legally reviewed**. **Nothing in it is legal advice and nothing has been reviewed by a lawyer.** Every legal statement below is a question for a lawyer, not a finding. The first country is **Germany**.

== Decisions taken

. **Lapis Cloud never holds money.** It does not receive, hold, forward or trigger a payment.
. **One currency per auction.** The currency cannot change after the first bid.
. **Bids only on the origin server.** Partner servers only *show* an auction; bidding, buying and paying happen on the origin server.
. **Sellers are members selling privately**, not the organisation.
. First country: **Germany**. Georgia later, checked separately.
. Today (0.56.0) auctions and tenders are paid **only in LTR**. A buy-now price already exists (`buyNowPriceLtr`, RPC `buyNow`, LTR only). A **price offer does not exist**.

== Implemented in wave (a) (V1.9.320)

Status: implemented and tested (H2 and PostgreSQL lane, including the races); **not legally reviewed**; the texts of both notices are the proposal of the authors, not a lawyer's wording.

*Scope.*

* `auction.currency` is `LTR` (every existing auction) or `EUR` (the only accepted code, `AuctionCurrencies.SUPPORTED`). A EUR auction carries its amounts as `Long` minor units (`starting_price_minor`, `buy_now_price_minor`, `final_price_minor`, bids in `auction_bid.max_bid_minor`); the LTR columns of such a row are `NULL` (CHECK `chk_auction_amount_columns`). Allowed range per auction: 1.00 to 100,000.00 EUR, minimum raise 1.00 EUR, at most 20 open EUR auctions per seller (there is no listing fee to deter spam).
* **A EUR auction never writes `ltr_ledger_entry`:** no new ledger row (no listing fee, no reservation, no sale booking); a listing-fee row from an earlier LTR phase of an auction switched to EUR stays. Creating a euro auction and switching LTR to EUR lock the member row (the open-auction limit), but never for a debit. `writeLedger` refuses a non-LTR auction (`check`), `AuctionFiatLedgerTripwireTest` scans the source, and the service test counts ledger rows around every operation.
* The award is computed by the same second-price proxy-bid rule as for LTR, in exact `Long` arithmetic (`computeFiatAuctionOutcome`, property-tested against the BigDecimal variant). Buy now works in EUR through the same `buyNow` RPC.
* Opt-in twice: the **instance switch** `organization_settings.auction_fiat_enabled` (off by default; an ADMIN switches it on only after acknowledging the *operator notice*, `AuctionFiatOperatorDisclaimer`; it is not part of `updateOrganizationSettings`) and a **seller notice** (`AuctionFiatSellerDisclaimer`) every member acknowledges once per version before listing in EUR. Both are versioned, SHA-256 hashed, echoed back unmodified and recorded append-only in `auction_fiat_acknowledgment` (kind `OPERATOR` / `SELLER`). An auction keeps the version it was created under.
* Switching the instance switch off blocks only *new* EUR listings; running EUR auctions stay biddable and settleable.
* Without `LAPIS_SECRET_ENCRYPTION_KEY` no EUR auction can be created (`AuctionFiatEncryptionUnavailableException`).

*Status flow.* `OPEN` to `AWAITING_PAYMENT` (award by time or by buy now), then the **seller** confirms "payment received" (`PAID`), the **winner** confirms "goods received" (`COMPLETED` once both exist). The two confirmations are independent, one-shot and need no order. Without a bid: `CLOSED_NO_SALE`. Nobody can cancel, there are no deadlines and no reminders; an award that is never paid stays `AWAITING_PAYMENT` (deliberate: Lapis arbitrates nothing, the screen says so permanently).

----
stateDiagram(name = "Status of a EUR auction (implemented)") {
    val start = initialState()
    val open = state(name = "OPEN")
    val awaiting = state(name = "AWAITING_PAYMENT")

    val completed = state(name = "COMPLETED")

    transition(source = start, target = open)
    transition(source = open, target = awaiting) { trigger = "end time with a bid, or buy now" }
    transition(source = open, target = unsold) { trigger = "end time without a bid" }
    transition(source = awaiting, target = paid) { trigger = "seller: payment received" }
    transition(source = paid, target = completed) { trigger = "winner: goods received" }
    transition(source = awaiting, target = completed) { trigger = "goods first, then payment" }
}
----

*Payment details (personal data of the seller).*

* Holder, IBAN (German, Mod-97 checked) and/or an `https` payment link are validated (`AuctionPaymentDetailsValidator`, a refusal names only the field, never the value) and stored as ONE `SecretBox`-sealed JSON blob (AES-256-GCM, `aad` = auction id) in the separate table `auction_payment_details`. They are in **no** `AuctionDto` (only the flag `hasPaymentLink`), in no list, no audit entry, no log and no exception message; `toString()` of every carrier is redacted.
* Only the **winner and the seller of an awarded EUR auction** can open them (`getAuctionPaymentDetails`); a stranger, a loser, an unawarded or an LTR auction and a missing id all answer the same `NotFoundException` with a constant message (no existence leak). The seller may replace them while the auction is `OPEN`; they are frozen from the award on. The currency and prices can change only before the first bid (`updateAuctionTerms`).
* Deletion: at once for `CLOSED_NO_SALE`; 30 days after the later confirmation for `COMPLETED`; 180 days after the award for an award that never completes; on erasure of a seller at once. `AuctionPaymentDetailsRetentionPoller` plus a lazy purge on read. The sealed blob travels in an organisation backup (`details_ciphertext`, listed in `BackupSecretColumnTripwireTest`); a restore on an instance with another key leaves it unreadable ("not available"), never an error with content.
* Audit: entity type `AUCTION`, snapshot `{event, currency, status, occurredAt}` only; the instance switch is audited as `ORGANIZATION_SETTINGS`.
* The client never caches the details, fetches them on a click, renders them as untrusted text and opens a link only when it still begins with `https://`, in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, naming its host.

*Not part of wave (a):* price offers, federation visibility, tenders, a second currency, an LTR-denominated dashboard signal for EUR auctions (the outbid/ending-soon signals stay LTR-only), a "sale withdrawn" status, any legal review.

== Model: direct payment outside Lapis Cloud

. The seller creates the auction with a currency, a starting bid, an end and **their own payment details** (for example an IBAN or their own payment link).
. The highest bidder wins. Lapis Cloud only establishes **who** won and **at which price**. Nothing is reserved or debited.
. The buyer pays **the seller directly**, outside Lapis Cloud. Lapis Cloud shows the payment details to the winner only; it triggers nothing and forwards nothing.
. In Lapis Cloud the seller confirms "payment received" and the winner confirms "delivered" (independent of each other). Lapis Cloud records nothing else.
. A dispute goes to the board or the arbitration body of the organisation. Lapis Cloud is not a party.

Not recommended: a payment provider integrated into Lapis Cloud (marketplace model; extra review, fees, and the question whether Lapis Cloud then sits in the payment chain), and any escrow in the system (that is holding money).

== Buy now and price offer

*Buy now (exists, LTR only).* An optional fixed price per auction; `buyNow` ends the auction at once at that price, guarded against concurrent bids by a row lock. For a fiat auction the fixed price is in the currency of the auction and is followed by the direct-payment flow, without any reservation.

*Price offer (new).* A buyer offers a price below the buy-now price. The seller accepts, declines or counters, each within a deadline (for example 48 hours). An acceptance ends the auction as a sale at the agreed price; an open offer does not extend the auction. Origin server only.

* Abuse limits: one open offer per buyer and auction, a cap of offers per auction, a block after repeated declines.
* When an offer binds the buyer and whether a consumer right of withdrawal applies to a commercial seller are **legal questions**.

----

== Data model (sketch)

* `auction.currency`: ISO 4217, mandatory, fixed after the first bid; amounts as integers in the smallest unit. LTR stays possible as its own "currency" `LTR` (today's behaviour, reservation only there). *(Implemented in wave (a) as `currency` plus `*_minor` columns.)*
* No `AUCTION_HOLD` and no entry in the LTR account for a fiat auction.
* The seller's payment details are personal data of a third party (GDPR), visible only to the winner, with retention and erasure rules.
* Confirmations "payment received" and "delivered" with a timestamp; the audit log holds no amount and no account number.

== Federation: visibility only

Template: the carpool exchange (`carpool-federation.adoc`): a signed teaser feed, consent per entry, a switch per partner, withdrawal with tombstones, hop limit, key pin.

* A partner shows only: title, starting or current price, **currency**, buy-now price, "price offer possible", end, origin server. **No** seller name, no bids, no offers, no payment details.
* Bidding and paying only on the origin server; the hint links there. An account on the origin server is needed.
* Consent of the seller per auction, off by default; instance switch and partner switch off by default.
* Open: whether the feed shows the running price or a static teaser, withdrawal on cancellation and end, protection against fake auctions, retention until the end of the auction.

== Legal questions for Germany (for a lawyer)

* Is a pure direct payment, with Lapis Cloud only documenting the award, outside the payment-services supervision (ZAG)? Does showing the seller's payment details change that?
* Private or commercial seller: repeated sales can be commercial (consumer law, VAT, trade registration, imprint). What notice does Lapis Cloud owe, and where does the responsibility lie?
* Platform reporting duties (Plattformen-Steuertransparenzgesetz, DAC7): is the organisation that runs the instance a platform operator, from which volume, what would Lapis Cloud have to record?
* Liability of the organisation for content and sales of its members (platform liability, Digital Services Act) and the terms of use.
* Anti-money-laundering: a threshold notice for higher amounts between private persons.
* Political parties: donation and reporting law (§ 25 PartG) if proceeds go to the party; for private sales between members probably not affected.
* Binding force of an offer and the offer flow.

== Waves (proposal)

. Currency per auction and the direct-payment flow, including buy now in fiat. **Done (V1.9.320), behind the instance switch, not legally reviewed.**
